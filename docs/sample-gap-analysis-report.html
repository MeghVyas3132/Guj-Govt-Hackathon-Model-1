Method, and what it cannot tell you

The boundary is tessellated into hexagons of 500 m edge length. Each camera contributes a footprint: types marked omnidirectional contribute a full circle of their nominal range, and directional types a sector spanning their recorded bearing plus or minus half their field of view. Ranges and fields of view come from the camera-type vocabulary, so they can be corrected without changing code. A cell's coverage is the area of its intersection with the union of all footprints, divided by the cell area. Cells at or above 60% are counted covered, at or above 20% partial, and below that a gap.

This estimate is deliberately conservative about what it claims. It is:

  • Two-dimensional. Elevation is not modelled.
  • Without terrain or building occlusion. A wall between a camera and a cell does not reduce the estimate, so real coverage is lower than shown here.
  • Based on nominal range per camera type, not on optics, sensor size, mounting height or lighting.
  • Dependent on the recorded bearing. 70 directional cameras here have no recorded azimuth or field of view and were treated as omnidirectional, which overstates their contribution. Recording bearings for those cameras is the cheapest way to make this report more accurate.

Treat these figures as a comparative planning aid — which zones are worse than others, and how much is recoverable by repair rather than purchase — not as a measurement of what any individual camera can see.
